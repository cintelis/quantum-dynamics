New to perovskites? How the pieces fit (30-second read)
ABX₃ is a crystal shape. B = lead (Pb) at the centre of cages, X = halide (iodide I, bromide Br, chloride Cl) making the cage walls, A = a small positive molecule sitting in the gaps. Example: MAPbI₃.
Cations (+) and anions (−) attract, so mixing salts like methylammonium iodide (MA⁺ I⁻) with lead iodide in solution and drying it grows the crystal. The halide mostly sets which colours of light are absorbed.
Big cations are spacers. Too big for the gaps, they slice the crystal into sheets n layers thick, like a club sandwich. Spacers repel water (more stable) but block charge (less efficient). 
[spacer] [Pb–I sheet × n] [spacer] [Pb–I sheet × n] …
Reading a formula: (PEA)₂MA₂Pb₃I₁₀ = two PEA spacers, an n = 3 sheet (2 MA + 3 Pb + 10 I). RP spacers have one charged end (pairs, with a gap); DJ spacers have two (one molecule bridges sheets, no gap).
?Sets the band gap the ranking aims for. The band gap is the lowest light energy a material absorbs: lower catches more sunlight, higher gives more voltage.
?Layered (quasi-2D): thin perovskite sheets separated by organic spacer molecules, usually more stable. 3D: the classic bulk crystal with no spacer.
?RP (Ruddlesden–Popper): the spacer has one charged end, so two spacer layers sit between sheets. DJ (Dion–Jacobson): charged at both ends, so one spacer bridges the sheets and they sit closer.
?The small ion inside the perovskite cage. MA = methylammonium. FA = formamidinium: more heat-stable, with a slightly lower band gap.
?I = iodide: lower band gap, absorbs more sunlight. Br = bromide: higher band gap, used for wide-gap and tandem top cells.
?How many lead-halide layers make up each sheet between spacers. Small n: thinner sheets, higher band gap, more stable. Large n: closer to 3D.
